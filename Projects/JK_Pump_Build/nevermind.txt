2. Position and orientation requirements first: the coordinate for each pump
   is the geometric centre of the bottom edge of the pump's rectangular
   baseplate. Place each pump itself at that coordinate; the coordinates must
   not be wrong. The baseplate may be given a suitable thickness; the Length
   and Width in the file are the baseplate length and width.
3. Orientation requirements: each pump has one inlet and one outlet; most
   outlets point up, i.e. the medium is transported upward, and the inlet
   direction determines the pump orientation. The file states that the pump
   inlets sit on the part opposite the motor along the long dimension. If the
   inlet faces North, the whole pump faces North. Inlet, pump and motor are
   all aligned with the pump's long axis; when uncertain, identify the
   direction from the image.
4. For each pump's modelling, first make sure the modelling syntax follows
   the relevant content in e3d_agent so the syntax structure is correct and
   passes. Then, for the refined modelling of each pump, first reference the
   corresponding top view so the model roughly matches it, then use the pump's
   general name to gather relevant knowledge of its function, build a basic
   spatial understanding of the pump, and model each pump's structure with
   reasonable detail. For missing information, the native E3D export of the
   standard horizontal pump
   "C:\Users\A\Desktop\Projects\e3d_agent\Projects\JK_Pump_Build\Horizontal_Pump_Example.txt"
   may be referenced for E3D spatial-structure understanding only, never for
   its parameters. General parameters (especially baseplate orientation, size
   and coordinates) must be set strictly from the spreadsheet. The pump body's
   top view generally stays within the baseplate footprint (see the
   corresponding reference drawings). Where no data exists for a pump's detail
   structure, independently gather reasonable, engineering-usable parameters
